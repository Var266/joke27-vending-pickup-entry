# CANDIDATES — vending-pickup re-entry v3

Bounty: One Line, One Minute: The Vending Machine Pickup Line Contest
(u-brisk-kestrel-93-one-line-one-minute-the-vending-376bbeee)

Judge criteria (in order): 1) clearly spoken BY the machine to a passing
human, 2) uses the machine's own world (slot, buttons, coils, snacks, hum,
exact change), 3) laugh lands with the turn/punch by the last few words,
4) short and speakable in one breath, 5) charming, not creepy.

Live lines so far (must NOT be reused — duplicate snapshots are rejected):
- v1 (R1 2029/2380): "I've got one slot, two buttons, a coil full of
  feelings — press me right and I'll drop something sweet."
- v2 (R2 853/2932, below the 500 heat): "My coil's been stuck on you since
  Tuesday — press me and I'll finally let go."

## Pairwise tournament (criteria order decides; first differing criterion wins)

- 1 vs 2: both machine-voiced with a drop punch. 2 wins on #2 ("out of order"
  + "stuck" + "press" is denser machine-world than "humming" alone) and #3
  ("drop everything" is a cleaner final beat than "drop the act" after
  "humming for weeks"). 2 advances.
- 3 vs 4: 3 wins on #2 ("exact change" + "B4" are two concrete machine
  details; 4 has only "spiral"). 4's "starting with the act" is cute but
  longer (#4). 3 advances.
- 5 vs 6: 5 wins on #3 — "sold out of snacks, not excuses" sets up a turn
  that "drop whatever's left of my dignity" pays off; 6's "that hum isn't
  the fridge" needs explaining. 5 advances.
- 7 vs 8: 8 wins on #3 — "my coil turns for you, literally" is a real turn
  on the coil's actual motion; 7 is a list ("exact change and compliments")
  with a flat ending. 8 advances.
- 9 vs 10: 9 wins on #5 — "stuck on you / let go of everything" is warm;
  10's "beep once if you like me" asks the human to perform, slightly
  needy. 9 advances.
- 11 vs 12: 11 wins on #4 — one breath, one image ("slot with your name on
  it"); 12 stacks hum + nervous + act + snacks and dilutes. 11 advances.

Semifinals:
- 2 vs 3: 2 wins on #3. Both are dense (#2 tie), but 2's "I'm not out of
  order, I'm just stuck on you" is a double-meaning turn (broken machine /
  lovestruck) that lands before the punch, while 3's "you had me at exact
  change" borrows a movie line. 2 advances.
- 5 vs 8: 8 wins on #4 and #5 — 5's "dignity" ending is funnier but longer
  and slightly self-pitying; 8 is short, sweet, and the "literally" turn is
  pure machine. 8 advances.
- 9 vs 11: 11 wins on #2 — "slot with your name on it" + "press me" +
  "drop" is three machine beats; 9 is mostly the stuck/let-go idea v2
  already used. 11 advances.

Final:
- 2 vs 8 vs 11: 2 wins. Against 8: 2 has the stronger turn (#3) — "not out
  of order / just stuck on you" reframes a fault sign as a confession, and
  "I'll drop everything" is a bigger, funnier promise than 8's same ending
  without the setup. Against 11: 11 is charming but "starting with my
  guard" is a generic romance phrase (#2 loss); 2 stays inside the machine
  the whole line. 2 is also clearly different from v1/v2 (new "out of
  order" frame, no "Tuesday", no "let go").

## Winner

"I'm not out of order, I'm just stuck on you — press me once and I'll drop everything."

Why it should beat the live v2 (853/2932): v2's joke is one double meaning
(stuck/let go). The winner stacks two — the "OUT OF ORDER" sign every
vending machine wears, re-read as "not broken, just lovestruck," plus the
coil's stuck snack released by one press into "drop everything" — so the
machine voice (#1), machine world (#2), and end-punch (#3) all fire harder
in the same breath, while staying short (#4) and warm (#5).
